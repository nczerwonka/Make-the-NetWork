# Broadband for Businesses

Connecting a business to a broadband network is much the same as connecting to any household (fixed wireless, fibre, DSL, or coaxial cable),
with infrastructure connected directly to the business or through a node. Businesses do, however, have a few extra factors to consider
when implementing broadband for their needs.

The primary concern is connection speed. The broadband needs of a business can vary wildly depending on the size of the company, their day-to-day
operations, and the industry in which the work. While the government of Alberta defines a 50 Mbps download as a basic connection
speed, businesses may require 100 Mbps or even up to 1,000 Mbps. The requirements of any business or very dependant on their own unique context,
and their needs should inform your decision as to how to connect them to your community's network. Additionally, broadband needs
today will not necessarily equate to broadband needs a year from now. The demand for higher and higher connection speeds continues to
rise, and businesses should anticipate and plan for potential increases to their connection needs down the road - the pandemic was 
an extreme example of this, which saw many businesses struggling to transition to remote work.

Businesses may also seek to enter into a service-level agreement (SLA) with their internet service provider, which is a binding
contract that can define things like connection quality, maintenance and repair response times, or connection uptime guarantees
in more explicit legal terms than household ISP agreements generally do.
[Read more on SLAs.](https://www.tbs-sct.canada.ca/pol/doc-eng.aspx?id=25761)
