# Broadband for the Home

Broadband can be connected to the homes within your community through any of the methods considered in this workshop
(fixed wireless, fibre) or by several older, 'legacy' methods (DSL, coaxial cable). Each method has their own benefits
and limitations which should be thoroughly researched and considered before deciding which type of broadband type to deploy
to the homes in your community.

An important consideration when planning home connections is whether or not broadband will be provided directly to each home, 
or if access will instead be connected to a community node, which then divides the connection amongst the homes within its network.

* This is especially important for fibre connections. Connecting fibre directly to the home will provide the fastest speeds,
but fibre-to-the-node (FTTN) connections allow fibre to be routed to a local cabinet, which then converts to DSL or coaxial cables
for the end connection to the home. This can help with material costs and quicken the speed on DSL and coaxial cables by shortening
their length, but will still be less efficient than a straight fibre-to-the-home connection.

Factors to consider when connecting broadband to the homes in your community are:
* Population density and expected network traffic
* Geographic density; i.e. feasability of a wired connection and associated material costs
* Possible node placement
* Legal regulators (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED), Rural Economic Development Canada, Canadian Radio-Television
 Telecommunications Commission (CRTC))
