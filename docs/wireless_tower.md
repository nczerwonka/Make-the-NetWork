# Wireless Tower

A wireless tower is a type of physical infrastructure that is essential to the creation of a fixed wireless network (FWA).
Wifi recievers in your home connect to your community's wireless tower via radio or microwaves, and the tower in turn
transmits information to a fixed location via either wireless or wired technologies.

These are some important points to consider when thinking about about wireless towers and fixed networks:

* Speed generally ranges from <20 Mbps to up to 100 Mbps on new 5g fixed wireless connections.
* Most wireless towers serve multiple fixed locations, which means that the overall seed of the connection has to be divided among the number of connections (for example, a 100 Mbps wireless connection serving 10 homes would provide 10 Mbps to each home).
* Wifi receivers must be within line-of-sight of the tower in order to connect, meaning that towers must be built to a certain height, and can be negatively impacted by topography such as hills, valleys, forests, and even weather.
* Considered ideal for remote and sparsely populated areas.

Your community will have to assess the costs versus beenfits for different types of tower construction (e.g., the height of the tower). 
You wil also have to consider how the tower is connected to your community point of presence (POP), whether it be
through microwave (wireless) or wired (fibre) technology. Generally, fibre fed towers are preferable, though more
expensive. Additionally, different connection types require different kinds of electronic equipment; their typical
maintenance costs should be compared to help make an informed decision.

Access to fixed wireless towers is becoming increasingly regulated by the government - specifically by Innovation,
Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED). Whether constructing a new tower or aiming to use an existing one,
there are several factors that need to be considered. Further information on tower sharing and construction can be 
found in ISED's Client Procedures Circular: [Radiocommunication and Broadcasting Antenna Systems.](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/learn-more/key-documents/procedures/client-procedures-circulars-cpc/cpc-2-0-03-radiocommunication-and-broadcasting-antenna-systems)

### Tower Radios

Depending on the needs of your network, and the geography across which you are building it, you may
need to make different decisions about the technology you use. In the case of building a fixed wireless
network via wireless towers, there are different types of equipment that are available to you which produce
different spectrum frequencies.

You have two different types of tower radios at your disposal; half are red, the other blue:
* The RED tower radio uses spectrum that has a large range, but has a difficult time travelling
  through obstacles such as trees or buildings, which will negatively effect the signal quality.
* The BLUE tower radio produces a stronger signal which can penetrate objects more efficiently than
  the signal produced by the red radio, but cannot travel nearly as far.

The structure and capabilities of your network will be impacted by the types of equipment you choose,
so it is important to consider the speciic needs of your community for this decision.
