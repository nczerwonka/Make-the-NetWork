# Broadband and the Environment

The construction and deployment of broadband infrastructure will have an impact on the land.
As a community it is instrumental that you understand the implications of developing this
infrastructure in natural areas, identify the parts of your land which need to be protected, 
and anticipate the logistical challenges that come with building a network while maintaining
good environmental stewardship.

Issues that may arise include:
* Physical habitat interferences and fragmentation
* Increased power consumption and carbon emissions
* Effects of climate on equipment
* Geographic difficulties (density, topology, line of sight)
* Distance to POP/availability of backhaul

The use of existing infrastructure to host broadband equipment (nimble networks) and broadband best practices (such as the 'dig once' construction policy) can mitigate
unnecessary environmental harm by reducing the amount of disruptive construction projects necessary to create a network.

Contractors are often required to develop and implement an [Environmental Construction Operations (ECO) Plan](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/9531ee58-3d58-402e-960e-363a5620539c/resource/1beb3d3f-08d3-4212-b410-8804bcf27cc5/download/trans-environmental-construction-operations-plan-framework.pdf)
to ensure compliance with Alberta regulations. These include mitigative steps for impacts such
as erosion and sediment destabilization, hazardous material usage, and dust management, among many others.
