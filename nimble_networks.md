# Nimble Mesh Networks

A nimble mesh network is a smaller, more mobile version of a fixed wireless network that would typically rely on wireless towers for its operation. Nimble mesh networks are created
by placing **nimble access points** on top of infrastructure that already exists, such as tall buildings, in order to mimic the role of wireless towers in a fixed wireless network.

You have been given two different types of **nimble access point** pieces, which are vital equipment for the construction of a nimble mesh network. The first comes with a connector
and must be connected to a wired network, the second can be placed freely as long as it resides within the footprint of at least one other access point. The third type of piece represents
the **footprint** of your nimble mesh newtork, or, the distance to which a **nimble access point** can communicate.

Important points to consider when building a nimble mesh network are:
* Just like wireless towers, **wireless nimble access points** must be within the footprint of each other in order to transmit information.
* Nimble footprints are smaller than those of wireless towers. When planning for use in your community, this should be considered.
* There are important factors to consider when choosing where to place a **nimble access point**, such as: what is the height of the building? How accessible is this building, and who would
  reasonably have access to the equipment? Are there weather exposure concerns?
